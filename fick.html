The premise

What if the Instagram stores around you delivered fashion as fast as groceries?

Groceries arrive in 10 minutes. A single outfit takes 20–30 minutes of scrolling, filtering, and second-guessing, but the real gap was on the other side of the transaction. Local stores in Gandhinagar, places like Pinky Fashion, had real stock and real style, but no way to sell beyond their own Instagram DMs.

Buyers wanted speed. Stores wanted reach. FICK wasn't a delivery app. It was infrastructure for a hyperlocal fashion community that didn't have one yet.

Built with Mrinmai (marketing), Teerth (systems), and Jay (photography): four people, one question, and a decision to actually try it.

Making it real

Finding the stores

These weren't random suppliers. They were local, often Instagram-only stores stuck taking orders through DMs with no way to reach anyone outside their own following. So the calls started.

Cold call · verbatim
Me“Hello bhaiya, RK Fashion se bol rahe ho? Meko ek baat poochni thi… aap online kapde bechte ho?”
Them“Online? Nahi… kyun?”
Me“Achha… toh aap kis type ke kapde rakhte ho? Casual, trendy, western? Aur aapko offline bechne mein koi dikkat hoti hai kya?”
Them“Tum ho kaun?”
Me“Nahi bhaiya, actually hum…”
Them“Abe maarunga! Zyada puchtachh mat kar!”
Call cuts immediately.

Most calls went like that. A few stores finally said yes.

The stock problem

The website said an item was available. The store had already sold it. Stock moved fast, racks changed daily, and there was no system to catch the gap.

The fix

A manual tagging system. Every item on the site got a tag: F0-16, F0-22, F0-31. When something sold, the vendor texted “F0-16 sold,” and it came off the site instantly.

The backend was a spreadsheet. Each piece: a tag, a number, a row. Not glamorous. It worked.

Going live

Learned Shopify from scratch in a week (store setup, infinite scroll, real-time stock) and shipped the first version after a week of trial and error.

Marketing & campaigns

Weekly reels, flyers, campus outreach, then brands started reaching out first.

All of it ran on one brand system: logo, hangtags, packaging, and campaign art, built to stay consistent across every reel, flyer, and collab.

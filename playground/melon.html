How the jelly works

Tetrahedra all the way down

The slice is a volume mesh of — particles joined into — tetrahedra, run with XPBD (extended position-based dynamics) at 60 Hz with 10 substeps. Every frame, each tetrahedron finds the rotation that best matches its current shape and is pulled softly toward its rest shape turned by that rotation. That is what lets the slice bend and twist like jelly without collapsing. Each tet also holds its volume, so a stretched slice thins in the middle.

The rind is up to three times stiffer than the flesh. Damping only slows the speed difference along each edge, so the slice falls and tumbles naturally while the wobble dies out, and once it is almost still the last tremor fades away. The bench pushes back with friction. When you grab it, a patch of particles is pulled toward your cursor, and pressing down near the bench gives way instead of crushing it.

The knife

Each piece remembers its outline in the flat, uncut shape. Your stroke sets a vertical blade plane. For each piece it crosses, the piece's current pose is fitted to a best rotation, and the plane is carried back into the rest shape, where it becomes a straight line. The outline is split along that line, and each half is rebuilt as a new tet mesh. Every new particle copies the position and velocity of the jelly it was cut from, so the halves carry on moving the way the whole was. Then the blade pushes them apart. Pieces bump into each other instead of passing through.

The skin you see

The smooth surface, seeds and air bubbles are separate meshes. Each of their vertices is pinned inside one tetrahedron by barycentric weights, so seeds stretch, turn and travel with the flesh around them. Colour comes from where a point sat in the original melon, so a fresh cut shows the right rind, pith and flesh.

Using your hands

“Use my hands” turns on your webcam and tracks 21 points on your hand with Google’s MediaPipe hand model, running entirely in your browser; no video leaves your device. Pinch your thumb and index finger to grab the jelly under the cursor, move to pull it, and open your fingers to let go. Hold your index and middle fingers out like a blade and swipe fast across the slice to cut along the swipe. The camera needs a normal web page; the claude.ai preview does not allow it.

Light through jelly

Each frame is drawn in three passes. First everything behind the jelly, then the depth of the jelly's back faces, which tells every pixel how much jelly its view ray crosses. The visible surface bends the background through that thickness, tints it by how much colour the flesh absorbs over that distance, adds a milky glow in the pale rind and at thin edges, and finishes with wet reflections of a soft studio.
